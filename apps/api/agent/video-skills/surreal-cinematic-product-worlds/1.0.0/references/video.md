# Continuous ownership across an impossible event

Start with believable contact, grip, posture, and item state. Use the approved
interaction to motivate the threshold. Match the same identity, product pose,
motion direction, and spatial anchor as the environment changes. A wipe,
light boundary, unfolding surface, or perspective reveal can carry the effect;
the reference's gold ring is not compulsory.

For the photographic treatment, keep skin, fabric, packaging, and surfaces
tangible while the surrounding event is impossible. If the brief explicitly
chooses animation or another medium, maintain that treatment consistently
instead of automatically converting it to live action.

A spatial reveal may use a push, arc, or reverse view, but it should preserve
one continuous physical body for each cast member. Keep group identities,
eyelines, hand ownership, and product assignments distinct. Effects should not
hide an unplanned item handoff or create extra copies of the hero.

Direct scale changes deliberately: establish the comparison, preserve design
proportions, and recover a legible interaction scale where use requires it.
Particles or secondary objects follow the chosen rule and a readable path.

Shift from spectacle to a tactile view that suits the category, then settle.
A bite, sip, application, control action, or material detail must preserve
physical state and remain connected to the marketed item. Stabilize the closing
silhouette and branding while the background moves quietly, if at all.

For native sound, use plausible contact and operation sounds with a concise
threshold accent when requested. Leave room for approved speech and honor
silence. The separate BGM role owns the score. Exact typography and final
compositing remain explicit production handoff requirements rather than claims
of work already executed.
