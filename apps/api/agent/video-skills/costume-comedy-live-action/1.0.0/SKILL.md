# Live-Action Costume Comedy

Translate period comedy into live-action footage with real actors, tangible
costumes, physical sets, and photographic materials. Preserve the source's
ceremonial grandeur, exaggerated actor expressions, comic reaction contrast,
and tactile product demonstration. Its animated rendering is not the target.

## Material and light

Use warm directional light, lacquer-red accents, dimensional gold detail,
deep fabric color, and separated highlights. Let embroidery, silk folds,
carved timber, and metal ornament have believable texture, weight, and wear.
Retain the actual hero item's shape, color, material, and authorized branding
even when its contemporary appearance creates an intentional period contrast.
Avoid an oversaturated flat wash that makes gilding and packaging merge.

## Expressive comedy

Exaggerated acting is welcome: wide eyes, raised brows, an open-mouthed pause,
an emphatic double take, sudden indignation, a broad delighted grin, or a
large but readable physical response. Scale these reactions to the comic beat
and give them a contrasting moment of stillness. Photographic realism does
not require subdued acting. Keep anatomy stable and expressions performable;
glowing pupils, elastic faces, and chibi proportions are not default effects.

## Formality into delight

Use ceremonial symmetry, social spacing and tactile detail to shape the
approved scene. A formal opening can contrast with a tighter reaction when
the story calls for that change. Give a planned product display readable
framing while retaining the comic personality. Preserve the approved plot; this visual style does not require
an emperor, a particular snack, or a death-threat punchline.

The intended final treatment is live action. Intermediate image assets follow
their current role contracts and requested rendering; this style adds no
illustration requirement to faces, costumes or environments. Maintain any explicitly supplied non-human identity rather
than silently recasting it as a human.
