# Product-Triggered World Expansion

Use a clear everyday action to open an extraordinary brand world. The
transformation can be spatial, material, or scalar; it is understandable
because the same product and a recognizable use relationship carry through it.

The selected audiovisual skill determines the rendering medium. This creative
method can support photographic effects, animation, illustration, or 3D when
requested, without inferring a medium from the presence of a human character.

## Why It Works

Reality gives the audience a starting point and a small human reason to watch.
A bite, touch, click, pour, or other appropriate interaction then becomes a
threshold. Keeping an identity, pose, object, motion, or spatial landmark
through the change makes the impossible event feel connected. Material detail
and actual use bring the experience back to something the viewer can desire.
A stable product finish makes the brand, rather than the effect alone, memorable.

This differs from a static surreal juxtaposition, an abstract sensory montage,
or a relay across unrelated worlds. The core is a product-motivated transition
from a readable everyday state into a coherent altered state.

## How To Execute

1. Extract the product identity, approved function or sensory quality, audience,
   occasion, and intended takeaway. Choose a short ordinary situation with
   a visible use relationship. The cast may be solo, a pair, a group, or
   partial-body coverage according to the brief.
2. Define one dominant world rule in a sentence: a product action opens space,
   changes apparent scale, or lets a supported material quality shape the
   environment. Derive it from this item rather than generic spectacle.
   Additional effects should express that same rule instead of replacing it.
3. Place the product in the grounded hook and give the change a clear trigger.
   Actual use may initiate the transition, continue within it, or resolve it.
   Do not repeat the use action merely to fill time. Preserve the mechanism
   appropriate to the category: opening, sipping, biting, applying, tapping,
   wearing, or operating are alternatives.
4. Design the threshold with a visible connection: match a gesture, silhouette,
   opening, direction of movement, light source, or foreground occlusion.
   Decide what remains stable and what changes. A light ripple, unfolding set,
   impossible perspective, or scale reveal can work; a glowing circular portal
   is optional. Give the audience a readable arrival state.
5. Explore the altered rule briefly. Keep the item recognizable through its
   proportions, materials, color, and authorized markings. If its size changes,
   establish a scale reference and a later ordinary-use scale. Preserve cast
   identities and their assigned items; multiple reference views are not new
   bodies. Plan deliberate transformations explicitly rather than allowing
   accidental geometry drift.
6. Reconnect the world to tangible product behavior with a plausible interaction
   or material close-up. A sip, texture application, real control gesture, or
   visible food detail can ground the fantasy. This need not return literally
   to the opening location. Keep any performance claim limited to supplied
   evidence; impossible scenery remains an expressive device.
7. For a 15-second brief, a useful starting allocation is about 0-3 seconds
   for the hook and trigger, 3-7 for transition and arrival, 7-11 for product
   detail or use, and 11-15 for the settled close. A 30-second version can
   develop anticipation and the same rule, rather than add unrelated worlds.
   Adapt the allocations to the actual narrative and duration.
8. End with stable product geometry, readable identity, and a clear use scale
   or explicitly composed hero scale. Let environmental motion quiet down.
   Add only approved brand wording, a supported closing line, or a requested
   CTA, with exact typography recorded in the production handoff.
9. Deliver a beat plan naming the grounded state, trigger, world rule,
   continuity anchor, changed state, honest product takeaway, and closing view.
   For each transition, state where the subject starts, what happens during
   the threshold, and where it arrives. Preserve clear narrative and product
   logic even if the camera path or effects treatment changes.
