# Composition and transition planning

Use the approved story to choose keyframes, then give each one a readable
foreground edge, subject position, and background anchor. Favor generous
negative space around the hero silhouette and enough environmental detail to
understand scale. Preserve product geometry and approved label design.

Keep the visual finish consistent across frames: pale architectural colors,
clear daylight, believable metal and glass, and controlled highlights.
Background figures, when required by the story, establish scale and remain
subordinate. Keep the established human representation consistent.

For each transition, record the ending pose and screen position, the next
starting pose, screen direction, camera side, and persistent landmark. If a
foreground element conceals a join, show a valid path around or through its
opening. Match geometry, light, and momentum on both sides of the occlusion.

Use stronger parallax for energetic beats and reduce visual activity near the
finish. Rotate the hero toward a recognizable final angle before holding it.
Allow printed branding to turn naturally with its surface instead of forcing
it to face camera during every rotation.

Check the sequence at thumbnail size: focus, geography, and the final brand
image should remain clear. Keep the storyboard layout required by the current
role; the style does not prescribe a new panel count or canvas arrangement.
