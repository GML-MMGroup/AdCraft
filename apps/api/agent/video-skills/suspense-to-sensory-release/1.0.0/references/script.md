# Performance and pacing

Apply pauses to the approved evidence and meaning changes. A short answer,
a listener stopping a routine gesture, or a held look can make an ordinary
statement feel unusually consequential. Play the setup sincerely; avoid an
early wink, comic mugging, or a label that announces the product category.

Let the reveal supply the explanation before the final dry callback. The
speaker can keep the same serious delivery while the surroundings become
ordinary. Do not rewrite the confirmed plot to add an interrogation or make
a psychiatric condition the joke. Preserve the approved dialogue and claims.

Budget speech at its actual performance pace with room for reactions. Trim
redundant exchanges in the authoring stage when authorized; do not rely on
rushed delivery to preserve every source line. Reserve readable time for
the demonstration and brand close. This reference directs spoken pacing,
not subtitle production or a new output schema.
