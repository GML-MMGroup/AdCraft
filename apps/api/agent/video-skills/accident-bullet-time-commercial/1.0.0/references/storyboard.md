# Show the cause before the airborne result

Keep the approved inventory, support states, visit order and complete interval.
Keep the first-frame footwear reveal through all objects landing in one video
segment and matching grid: unequal-time samples, not cuts or a first/last-frame target.

## Make the contact frame useful

Choose independent walking routes, headings, contact side and exit before contact.
For the reference pattern, a passerby's back/shoulder enters a foreground edge and
continues into depth on an opposing offset lane. The bodies must reach the same
contact plane: shoulders actually meet, with visible fabric compression or a small
torso deflection. Keep that touching edge readable, not hidden by a large foreground
head/back. Do not show a separate passerby introduction or force face visibility;
gaze and stride follow each destination, without swerving around the protagonist.

Sample the actual contact, not its finished reaction. Show the supported originals
and the first asymmetric weight transfer: torso checked slightly back, one knee
yielding, hands beginning a recovery reflex. Do not replace this with both palms
already posed open and all objects hovering. The next sample supplies the airborne
result. A still panel cannot show contact, detachment and apex as three simultaneous
states of each object; action prose connects them in time.

For the usual nine-panel incident, use:
1. Footwear-height first frame, low backward tracking; off-crop items are supported.
2. Continuous rise establishes initial support and the chosen approaching lane.
3. Readable shoulder contact and onset of balance loss; the initial impulse starts.
4. After release: original items decelerating into a shared freeze, empty supports,
   intermediate medium-to-close approach toward the first object, no long group pose.
5-7. Connected near-to-far visits; outgoing close edge and smaller deeper next target
   carry perspective through each transition, including arrival at the main product.
8. Continuous pullback reconnects the same frozen event and prepares resumed gravity.
9. All originals grounded and settled with the approved body reaction.

## Transfer the physical states, not a floating arrangement

Track support, dislodging motion, departing direction, frozen place and landing.
Show grip loss or a loose support slipping; attachments cannot pass through the
body. An initial impulse precedes forward/upward flight and deceleration, not a
slow rise from still hands. Move the camera to support-derived heights rather
than hoisting everything above the face. The vacated support stays empty.
One original per panel, with attached parts and closures intact.

Freeze the recovery gesture, head and gaze until time resumes. A brief slight
backward recoil is not a fixed-feet backbend. Preserve identities and selected
medium; reference carrying poses do not override later possession. Splashes need
an already open source. Show depth within the bounded A/B/C corridor, not only a
blurred background, equal-height row or three isolated product portraits.

## Keep the tour continuous

The camera travels forward through fixed world space; height/lateral offsets follow
release positions. The outgoing item enlarges past an edge while the already visible,
smaller deeper target grows into its visit. Accelerate between visits, slow briefly
for detail, then continue. Camera roll and tilt share that path, with common landmarks
and parallax. Each item becomes dominant; the advertised product receives the
strongest view. No independent orbit, zoom reset, hidden cut or backward detour.
Only the final pullback retreats; leave two to three seconds for complete landing.

Record the causal opening, continuous path and landed end_state in the handoff.
The grid is an off-screen story-process reference; Video starts in the full-frame
scene. No dialogue lettering, bubbles or labels; retain packaging, logos and
environmental text. Video subtitles follow their own control. Resolve conflicts
in this call, without a visual-model gate or media rerun.
