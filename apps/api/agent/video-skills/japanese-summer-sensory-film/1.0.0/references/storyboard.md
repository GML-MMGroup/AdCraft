# Sensation, action, and a legible finish

Use the required nine-panel grid to describe the approved progression rather
than demand nine separate cuts. Mix environmental context, tactile detail,
movement or pause, actual use, a small response, and a hero view. Each panel
should add information instead of repeating the same smiling mid-shot.

Specify visible identities, positions, gaze, hand ownership, and the assigned
item. Track grip, closure, liquid level, and any transition from sealed to
opened. Retain the same physical body through adjacent views; reflections and
repeated panels do not introduce extra cast. Group shots preserve distinct
identity and wardrobe assignments.

Choose framing to make the sensory cue and product action readable. Low sun
and backlight can support a moving moment; a tighter held view can clarify
contact or opening. Grid compositions remain guidance for the final footage,
which can find a natural camera path while preserving the approved action.

Reserve a calm ending view with clear merchandise and space for authorized
brand text if needed. Keep panels text-free and record exact lettering,
placement, and readable hold time in the designated handoff fields.
