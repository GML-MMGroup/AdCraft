# Movement and contact

Favor slow lateral travel, small push-ins, and controlled pullbacks over
constant orbiting. Let a deliberate gesture and its visible result determine
the shot length. Set one primary action and one camera path per segment.

Powder should fall from its visible source, briefly hang in light, then settle.
A brush bends against a surface; a viscous coating remains where applied;
piped material grows from the nozzle and retains its deposited shape. Use
these cues only for actions already approved in the shot.

For miniature staging, keep scale and limb proportions stable. Tools contact
the correct surface, ladders have support, and effort points in the direction
of movement. An ingredient passing close to the lens may conceal a join, but
its position and rotation must connect to the next state. Avoid morphing
figures, sliding feet, multiplying berries, or regenerating untouched surfaces.

Settle into the final display with enough visual stillness for recognition.
If native environmental sound is requested, use delicate synchronized
sifting, bristle contact, soft crumb movement, or gentle tool contact. Keep
silent segments silent and follow the established audio mode.
