# Uncanny Testimony Reveal

Translate the supplied story's double meanings into a product-led method.
The reusable element is a sincere witness, a questioning listener, withheld
context, and evidence that becomes ordinary at the reveal. The clinic,
doctor, patient, dinner table, winter, exact clock time, and food are optional.

## Why It Works

The audience supplies the alarming explanation; the witness supplies facts
that remain true after the reveal. Humor comes from disproportionate gravity,
not from announcing that the witness lied. The final demonstration pays off
both the mystery and the product's value, rewarding a second viewing.

Unlike broad Misdirection and Reversal, this method specifically uses testimony
and questioning to accumulate dual-meaning product evidence. Unlike Genre
Hijack, it needs that evidence to resolve, not just borrowed suspense grammar.
Do not add those parent methods as redundant selections. Its audiovisual
treatment is independent: the story can work in animation or bright deadpan
staging as well as restrained suspense.

## How To Execute

### Start with facts and the reveal

Identify the product, audience, one desired benefit, approved claims, visible
or audible behavior, usage context, and target length. Mark an unverified
capability as an assumption to resolve; do not promote it into a fact.
For a service, use a supported interaction and visible outcome. If the
benefit is abstract, ground the evidence in a real user ritual or interface.

Choose the actual use shot that can explain the mystery before writing the
questions. Work backwards to two or three distinct, filmable clues.

### Build the evidence table

For every major clue record:

| Verified fact or supplied routine | Witness wording | Audience inference | Reveal action | Benefit connection |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A configured vacuum starts on schedule | It wakes when the room is empty | An unseen visitor | Show the scheduled start and the same motor sound | Less manual cleaning |
| It passes below a supported furniture clearance | It knows what is under the sofa | A creature searching | Show the same path and collected crumbs | Reaching that cleaning area |

Examples illustrate the mapping, not claims about every model. Remove a clue
that cannot be explained by the selected product. Material change, movement,
sound, touch, recurring use, interface feedback, and subjective feeling are
available axes; a product does not need all of them or a literal sound.

Use metaphor to change interpretation, not physical truth. A crop can hide
a sound's source; a real monster, injured person, or impossible disappearance
cannot be quietly explained away by showing an ordinary product later.

### Cast functions, then choose the setting

Use a witness who knows the experience and a listener who asks the questions
the viewer needs. Friends, housemates, a colleague, an interviewer, or a
service desk can fill those functions. Select a setting natural to the
category. Keep the original clinical staging only when requested and apt;
the comic target is the misunderstanding, not a psychiatric diagnosis.

### Escalate meaning, not word count

Progress from an unexplained occurrence to a concrete behavior, then to the
witness admitting they want the experience again. Each question should add
evidence or change the listener's interpretation. Cut repetitive questions
about timing, fear, or location that do neither.

End the testimony on an unfinished or ambiguous phrase whose real referent
the next shot reveals. The phrase can concern relief, convenience, comfort,
precision, pleasure, or a habit; do not default to hunger or addiction.
The original recurring time is a person's routine, not a universal feature.

### Budget the ending before the dialogue

Use a flexible allocation: roughly one fifth to establish the mystery, one
third to deepen it, one fifth to the emotional contradiction, and the rest
to demonstration, callback, and brand recognition. For a sixty-second brief,
reserve approximately twelve to fifteen seconds for the reveal and close;
adjust to the approved delivery rather than enforcing these proportions.

Estimate spoken duration at the intended language and performance pace, then
include pauses, reactions, and product action. Compress repeated exchanges
instead of accelerating every line. Short versions may use one strong clue;
longer versions should add evidence, not a longer interrogation.

### Pay off the evidence and the brand

Reveal the source of the strongest sound, gesture, or phrase first. Show the
other decisive clue in the next action or composition. The ordinary context
must explain what the viewer previously heard or saw, including any exact
time that mattered. A brief straight-faced callback confirms the witness
was accurately describing a good experience all along.

Close on the actual product, supported service experience, or approved brand
identifier with a benefit-specific line. Do not automatically copy the source
tagline, package, logo, or promise that the viewer cannot stop consuming.

### Deliver and audit

Use the existing capability output contract. Include the concept, selected
facts, evidence mapping, adapted character functions and setting, timed beats,
reveal action, and benefit callback in its available authoring fields; this
skill does not introduce API fields or replace required schemas.

Read each clue again after the reveal: is it still accurate, and is its
meaning now clear? If any unrelated product could replace the final packshot
without changing the testimony, rebuild the clues. Keep optical treatment,
sound production, and runtime settings with their respective owners.
