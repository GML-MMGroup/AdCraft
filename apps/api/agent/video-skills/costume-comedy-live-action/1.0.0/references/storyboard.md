# Hierarchy, experience, and reaction coverage

Make the approved relationship readable through position, height, eyelines
and formal framing. An architectural edge can conceal a furtive action when
that beat exists. Choose reaction coverage for the actual cast and exchange;
a commanding authority and subordinate response are optional story content.

When a demonstration is in the approved sequence, give it a clear insert: the actual opening mechanism,
surface, tactile action, or useful output. Choose a close reaction that clarifies the approved emotional change;
suspicion turning to delight is one possible beat. A wide-eyed
pause or exaggerated jaw drop is compatible with real actors. Let the
reaction register before the next cut.

Move from formal separation to a shared or welcoming composition when the
approved story resolves. When the sequence owns the commercial ending, use a stable hero display
and sufficient space for the selling message in the later edit. Keep generated panels
free of typography; put approved benefit and CTA wording in the appropriate
script or editing fields. Preserve the required nine-panel format and adapt
coverage to the current sequence; the grid does not prescribe nine cuts.

Maintain screen direction, costume, hands, package orientation, and any
before-and-after product state. Do not duplicate the source's animated
eye beams, face distortions, cast proportions, or floating decorative clouds.
