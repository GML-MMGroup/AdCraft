# One recognizable person before and after the mishap

Default to designing the protagonist only. An anonymous passing extra can cause
contact without a separate Character Main, Turnaround or identity binding.
Describe their brief silhouette and movement in the shot action, not a second
design proposal. Preserve explicitly selected cast and approved speaking or
recurring roles; never remove a supplied identity or invent a subplot to add one.
Even for a designed supporting character, the reference establishes identity,
not a duty to show the face. Back, side, shoulder or partial views are valid;
natural frontal visibility may follow an approach from behind the protagonist.
Do not turn or stop the passerby for identification. Respect explicit person limits.

Preserve the approved identity, hair, outfit, footwear and accessories across
all references. Use the existing Main and full-body multi-view contracts.
References establish anatomy, proportion and usable clothing construction;
they do not need to enact the accident or contain flying products.
The carrying pose is a starting state, not a permanent identity feature. Use the
bound product reference for the advertised item's design rather than an
incidental substitute in a character sheet. Later release or pickup changes
possession without changing the wearer's identity or creating another item.

Establish carrying relationships for the three distinct belongings: which hand
holds each item, which item is worn, where a
bag strap rests, which side wears an accessory, and how fingers can release a
held item. Use available hands and real attachment points; three items do not
mean three hands. Release follows contact, never an unexplained accessory jump.
Keep a phone's screen and grip functionally oriented. Preserve separate
identities when a passerby is independently designed; an anonymous extra is not
another depiction of the protagonist. Do not duplicate bodies in close views.

For shot performance, use an everyday walk followed by slight backward recoil
and instinctively opening hands after contact; the original belongings toss away.
Keep both palms empty and the body looking toward those airborne items during
the tour. A low look or late reach can follow when time resumes. Match the
narrative handoff; falling is optional and the accident pose does not belong in
identity reference sheets. The story does not require a specific gender, body type, pink dress
or fashion pose. Keep explicitly stylized characters in their selected medium;
realistic identity references do not determine the final film medium.
