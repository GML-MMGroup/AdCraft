# Suspense to Sensory Release

Make an approved tonal reversal felt through contrast in framing, light,
rhythm, and sound. The signature is withheld sound sources followed by
source-revealing cuts, with deadpan performance across the transition.
Unlike a sustained psychological thriller, the tension opens into readable,
desirable product experience rather than a darker ending.

## Two compatible visual states

For the suspense state, use restrained color, motivated side light, contained
compositions, selective focus, and quiet negative space. Preserve faces and
important gestures in shadow. Cool gray, muted green, or neutral charcoal
are options, not a mandatory clinic palette. Fewer camera moves make a small
lean, stopped gesture, or held look meaningful.

For the release state, increase spatial clarity and material separation.
Open the framing, recover natural product color, and reveal contact, surface,
or interaction details. Warmth suits some products; clean neutral light can
suit technology, fabric, or a digital service better. Desire may mean comfort,
precision, quiet, or ease, not necessarily appetite or exuberance.

## A perceptible hinge

Coordinate the visual and sonic change around the approved reveal action.
An optional brief blackout, a held reaction, a match cut, or a direct source
reveal can supply the hinge; do not stack them all. Keep one connecting cue
across the cut so the contrast reads as an explanation. A sound initially
heard without context can become an ordinary device, material, or action
when its source is seen. For a naturally quiet product, use a visual or
tactile cue rather than inventing noisy machinery.

Use intentional stillness before the hinge and more decisive demonstration
after it. The finish settles enough for product recognition and a dry comic
response. Avoid constant jump scares, arbitrary flash cuts, universal red
lighting, or making every category glossy, steaming, and edible.

## Scope and progressive disclosure

Apply these choices to the approved story and selected representation.
Do not introduce a clinic, diagnosis, monster, dialogue exchange, product
claim, or new plot simply because the style is selected. A scene before or
after the reveal receives its local treatment; every asset or segment does
not need both states. Preserve identities and actual product geometry.

Load only the current role reference: script for performance pacing,
character for appearance, product or prop for material treatment, scene for
environment, storyboard for framing, video for movement and authorized
speech or synchronous sound, and bgm for instrumental score. Audio execution
follows the project's audio mode; visual rhythm can carry the contrast in a
silent film. No particular duration, provider, or rendering medium is required.
