# Direct an energetic continuous camera path through frozen space

Use the approved item map and order. Keep collision through all objects landing
in one video segment: contact/release, shared freeze, all visits, reconnection,
resumed gravity, every ground contact and settling. Keep two to three seconds
for descent/consequence inside the confirmed duration and current fifteen-second
cap. Extra setup or payoff may use other nodes; the action remains complete.
At time zero establish the initial support state before contact: items are still
held or worn, and later airborne panels cannot stand in for that opening.
Show independent walking routes with gaze and torso following each person's
destination. Limited clearance produces a brief outer-shoulder brush and passive
recoil; the passerby keeps their travel direction rather than turning to strike.
Make contact precede release and leave enough time to read that cause.

For each visit specify lens position, approach, dominant subject, real detail,
readable dwell and exit. Describe a continuous camera path with strong depth
travel and changing height. Accelerate toward an item, decelerate for inspection,
then accelerate past its edge toward the next. Fast travel remains trackable;
use restrained optical motion blur in transit and crisp detail during the dwell.
Continuous camera roll, orbit and angle changes remain valid; vary speed and
orientation along the same path.
For the first medium-to-close bridge, describe intermediate approach views:
the released item grows gradually while the wearer and landmark slide out with
continuous parallax. Travel visibly around its edge before showing a new side.
Keep that passage on screen, rather than substituting an isolated product insert
at the moment of release. Use the same bridge logic between later visits.

Let each item dominate its visit, often filling most of the useful frame or
extending beyond one edge. Background people and other belongings can leave the
frame. At a bridge the outgoing item passes close to an edge and disappears,
revealing new foreground/background overlap. Move the lens through the empty
space beside objects, never through their solid geometry. Use an identifiable
landmark for parallax without holding it upright or in the same screen position.
Preserve reference-backed object scale; apparent enlargement comes from proximity.

Subject time and camera motion are independent: all frozen positions, body poses,
closures, liquid shapes and droplets remain fixed in world space while the camera
moves. Preserve an expressive suspended state created before freeze. Use splashes only for established open vessels.

Freeze head and gaze too; new looks or reaches begin after time resumes.
The vacated support stays empty after release, through the tour and landing;
an offscreen item retains its mapped state. A bag and its attached strap are
one object. Product references govern its design, character sheets its wearer's
identity, and the approved action its current support and location. Follow the
shared actor-relative map instead of reconstructing incompatible grid poses.
At pickup, move the grounded object into the hand and leave its old spot empty.

Allocate time to the actual route: a readable supported opening and release,
fast travel with brief detail dwells, a short reconnection and complete landing.
Give the advertised item the strongest emphasis within the accepted duration.

Maintain one spatial journey without cuts, dissolves or viewpoint replacement.
Review viewpoint, scale and speed contrast alongside continuity. Reaching,
leaning or an optional body fall must fit the next segment's start_state.

The grid is an off-screen story-process reference, not a first/last-frame target.
Start at time zero inside the full-frame scene. Do not show sheet layouts, borders
or zoom into a panel. Use event order without exact panel framing/timing locks.
Record the landed end_state and camera pose. Preserve speech and subtitle choices;
Video owns contact/landing foley and ambience, while BGM remains separate.
