# Doctor-Patient Reversal

Use this clinic-specific alternative to **Uncanny Testimony Reveal** when
the brief calls for doctor-patient suspense turning into comedy. It is
self-contained; selecting the parent as well is unnecessary. The method
fixes the premise and payoff, not a stock plot, product or set of questions.

## Why It Works

The audience initially reads the patient's account through the seriousness
of the consultation. A late verbal turn earns the doctor's comic instruction
without identifying the commercial source. A brief functional cutaway can
make the experience understandable before the clinic callback. The final brand freeze reveals
that source and changes the meaning of the evidence rather than declaring
it false. The joke works as dialogue; the product makes the clues click.

## How To Execute

### Keep the premise, invent the product story

Keep the dialogue in one medical consultation room with exactly three distinct characters:
a doctor, a nurse and a psychiatric patient. The doctor leads the questions,
the patient sincerely describes the experience, and the nurse listens or
assists and visibly receives the doctor's closing instruction. The nurse
need not speak or appear in every shot. Preserve their identities throughout;
do not add other patients, visitors, an announcer or another dialogue location.
An optional late functional cutaway and the final product still may use
settings appropriate to the benefit. These inserts add no new speaking role;
return to the same consultation room for the final dialogue and instruction.

Begin with the supplied brand story, one supported product benefit and the
real experience that could be misunderstood. Design the final brand still
before writing the conversation: its product or service identifier and
concise benefit must connect the decisive clues to the advertised item.
An earlier functional insert may reveal the effect, but not its commercial source.

### Make the setup fair and commercially invisible

Map each important clue as: **true product fact or use experience -> patient's
ambiguous wording -> alarming audience inference -> optional functional evidence
-> final brand-frame identification**.
Choose enough distinct clues for the requested length. Each doctor question
must uncover evidence or alter the interpretation, not merely repeat fear,
time or place. The patient's sincere answers carry the mystery.

The advertised item's first on-screen appearance is the final brand freeze.
Until then, exclude that item, its recognizable silhouette or packaging,
identifying interface, brand name, logo and promotional text, including in
backgrounds, reflections, worn accessories and in-room screens. Keep the
opening ambiguous. In the later part, the benefit or category may become
inferable from functional evidence; preserving suspense does not require
hiding every effect until the end. The specific product and brand supply
the last connection. Do not invent events the supported benefit cannot explain.

### Optionally show the effect, then return to the room

After sufficient buildup, a short cutaway outside the clinic may show a
real function, mechanism, sensory change or result connected to the patient's
account. It is explanatory evidence, not a separate story or an early packshot.
Show what changes without revealing the marketed item that causes it.

For the user's windshield-fluid reference, water, wiper movement and a
clearing windshield are allowed functional imagery; the marketed bottle,
packaging and brand stay hidden. This boundary depends on the actual brief:
if the wiper itself is the advertised item, its recognizable blade cannot
be the early insert. Choose evidence that keeps that item off screen.
Do not replace the hidden item with a generic or unbranded lookalike demo.

Use a cutaway only if it clarifies a real clue. Connect it through the
patient's approved words, a visual association or a matching sound, then
return to the clinic for the final exchange and doctor's instruction, with
an optional patient reaction if useful.
Do not cut directly from the functional passage to the brand finish or force
an external insert into a story that already works through dialogue alone.

### Turn the dialogue, then let the doctor deliver the joke

Distinguish the verbal turn from the product reveal. A late answer changes
the doctor's reading of the encounter and earns the comic instruction, while
the exact commercial source stays withheld. If a functional cutaway is used,
return to the three characters before completing the final exchange.
The final brand frame identifies the product behind the clues. Its specific
benefit must explain why the earlier answers were reasonable; a replaceable
logo after an unrelated joke is not a resolution.

Choose one closing instruction, spoken by the **doctor to the nurse**:

- **Discharge:** the patient's verbal clarification reassures the doctor without
  naming or showing the product; the doctor calmly instructs the nurse to
  arrange discharge. Use the user-supplied line
  "办理出院" for a Chinese-language script.
- **Increase the dose:** the patient's final ambiguous enthusiasm, boast or
  callback provokes a deadpan response; the advertised item and brand are still withheld.
  Use the user-supplied line "加大剂量" for a Chinese-language script.

For another requested dialogue language, preserve the same meaning. Earn
the selected line through the preceding exchange; do not append it at
random or routinely combine both. The joke concerns the misunderstanding
and reaction; it does not claim the advertised product treats psychiatric
illness. No medical explanation or actual dosing scene is needed.

### Optionally add a patient reaction

After the final exchange and the doctor's instruction, choose whether a
patient reaction improves the joke, characterization or pacing. It is optional,
independently of the functional cutaway. Omit it when the dialogue already
lands, and cut directly to the brand freeze without reserving an extra shot.
If included, give this fictional patient's
established unusual mental state a specific behavioral expression, rather
than a generic satisfied-consumer smile: a slightly mistimed grin, an oddly
held look or a small repeated gesture can echo the preceding dialogue.
Choose an action motivated by this story; these are options, not a fixed
routine or diagnostic signs for real patients. Keep the beat intelligible
and comic, without adding another line that reveals the product or a new subplot.
Fit its duration to the brief rather than copying the source timestamps.

### Land on a real brand freeze

After the instruction, or after the optional patient reaction, cut directly to the final frozen
brand introduction. This is the first product appearance and the commercial
reveal, not a reminder of something already shown. Place the actual product
or service identifier in a credible setting connected to its use and the clues.
Choose the environment and supporting surface for that product, with believable
scale and placement. Avoid a generic empty backdrop or forcing it onto a
clinic desk merely to preserve the dialogue location. The reference's car
hood at a city roadside illustrates context for windshield fluid; it is
not a required setting for other categories.

At the first frame of the freeze, show the exact approved brand name as large,
prominent text alongside the product. Keep it visible throughout the hold;
small packaging print, a logo alone or a later title does not fulfill this beat.
Keep a concise supported benefit legible as secondary copy.
A supplied offer or CTA is optional. Use the still to resolve the clues,
not merely display a generic slogan. Reserve enough hold time for viewers
to understand the connection. Any functional cutaway belongs before the final
clinic exchange, not after the closing instruction or optional reaction.

Keep the dialogue action in the room; the final contextual product still is
a brand finish, not another dialogue scene. For multi-segment production, the arc spans
the complete film and only the final segment carries this closing freeze.
Audit every earlier shot, line and overlay for accidental item or brand disclosure;
do not reject a late functional insert merely because the benefit is understandable.
Separate product reference assets remain complete and accurate for production;
their existence or connection as inputs does not authorize early screen presence.
