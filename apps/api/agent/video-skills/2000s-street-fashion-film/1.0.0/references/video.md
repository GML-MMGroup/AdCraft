# Photographic movement and alternating pace

Render final footage as live action: stable facial identity, tangible skin,
hair, fabric, glass, and leather, natural daylight, restrained grain, and soft
highlight roll-off. Preserve the actual product color and structure. A reference
image's rendering treatment is not a request to make the final footage drawn.

Use short lateral follows, observer-like handheld drift, a brief head turn,
and motivated changes of camera height. Let a passing object briefly occlude
the frame; a new view reveals the same continuous action without leaving a
duplicate body behind. Reflections belong to their physical model. Maintain
distinct models and their individual outfits when they converge or cross.

Alternate brisk transit and detail fragments with longer portraits, readable
full looks, or street breaths. Slow a gesture or held look selectively when the
approved timing permits it; keep walking, foot contact, grips, strap swing,
fabric weight, and hair response plausible. Brief optical softness is a
transition accent, not a persistent loss of face or merchandise detail.

City cutaways need actual movement: traffic, a changing signal, rising steam,
wind, or shifting occlusion. Return through a matched action, light, or framing
anchor. End in a steadier view that gives the later brand signature room.

For native sound, use restrained street ambience and synchronous footsteps,
cloth movement, or hardware contact when requested. Keep supplied speech
intelligible and speaker-specific; otherwise do not invent a sales monologue.
Respect requested silence. The separate BGM role supplies the score. Exact
typography and finishing remain explicit postproduction handoff items.
