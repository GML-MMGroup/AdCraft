# Score the contrast between everyday time and the suspended instant

Design an instrumental energy curve around the approved whole-film event:
a light forward pulse for ordinary motion, a concise change at impact, a spacious
sustained or half-time texture during product inspection, then a short resolving
accent when ordinary speed returns. Leave room for the contact and landing
foley; avoid a dense barrage of accents that makes the whole film uniformly busy.

Keep the suspended passage inviting and commercially polished rather than
turning a small mishap into a disaster cue. Match timbre to the product and
selected medium. A warm electronic bed, restrained bass and fine high textures
are options, not an obligatory genre or tempo. Preserve approved silence.

Use the project's actual event windows. If exact hit-point synchronization
requires editing, record those target events as a handoff rather than claiming
the generated track will already align frame-perfectly. Video owns native
footsteps, contact, spill, landing and speech; the BGM prompt does not duplicate
them or add vocals unless separately authorized by the current audio contract.
