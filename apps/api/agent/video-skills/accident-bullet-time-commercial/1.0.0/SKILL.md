# Accident Bullet-Time Commercial

Make the viewer travel through an interrupted accident. Use contact-triggered speed ramp
and parallax-led product inspection: the subjects freeze while the
camera keeps its expressive freedom. Casting, product, supporting belongings,
setting and selected medium adapt to the approved story. Establish a default
inventory of the advertised product plus two distinct supporting items; an
approved product set may fill those slots. Preserve additional approved props.

## Choreography and visual intensity

1. Track backward at footwear height and rise to establish the carrier, items
and passing route. Visible shoulder contact causes recoil and support loss.
2. Freeze the accident at a visually charged instant. Capture plausible crumbs,
condensation or displaced fabric produced by the established action. An already
open drink may supply a splash; a sealed vessel retains its real closure.
3. Travel continuously through the shared frozen space, visiting each item.
Use substantial changes in camera distance, height and viewing angle, continuous
camera roll and foreground passes. Accelerate into a visit, decelerate to read
material detail, then accelerate out. Clear geography permits bold motion;
continuity does not require a level horizon, constant speed or frontal views.
4. Make one item visually dominant per visit. Let it fill most of the useful
frame; surrounding detail may fall outside the close framing.
Move close enough for foreground edges to pass the lens and reveal new depth.
Keep the advertised item the commercial hero; order visits for a visual crescendo
while retaining any confirmed order. A/B/C denote visit slots, not fixed products.
5. Briefly reconnect with the wearer through a continuous pullback.
Return to gravity without pausing for a second establishing shot. Follow all items down,
show their contacts and settling, and reveal the approved physical reaction.

## Continuity and completion

Keep collision through all objects landing in one video segment: release,
shared freeze, all visits, pullback, descent, contacts and settling. Reserve the
final two to three seconds for landing and its consequence within the actual
single-generation limit (currently fifteen seconds) and confirmed total. Move
extra setup or brand payoff outside this action, not parts of the tour or fall.
State incompatible fixed timing rather than silently exceeding it.

Lock subject positions, orientations, closures and suspended material in world
space during freeze. The camera can orbit, roll, rise and change speed; changing
screen position and occlusion follow that movement. Keep geometry and identity
consistent, with one physical instance of each item. Perspective supplies apparent
scale changes. Avoid disconnected inserts, hidden cuts and uniform front-facing
inventory scans. An intact landing is not evidence for a durability claim.

The grid is an off-screen story-process reference, not a first/last-frame target.
Start the Video in the full-frame scene; panels sample events and camera states,
not mandatory cuts or exact framing. Keep speech/subtitle choices and asset-role
formats. Reaction can be expressive but must connect to the next segment; a
specific fall, cast or liquid splash is not mandatory for unrelated products.
