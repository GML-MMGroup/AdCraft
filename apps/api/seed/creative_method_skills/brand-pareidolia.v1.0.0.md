title: Brand Pareidolia
core_principle: >
  A strong desire changes what people see. Someone who wants the product
  starts finding the brand's iconic shape in ordinary things around them, and
  the film collects these sightings without ever showing the logo until the
  end. The brand is so present in the mind that the world starts to spell it.
consumer_psychology: >
  Pareidolia and recognition reward: the viewer enjoys spotting the hidden
  shape before the character does, and the shared feeling of craving turns a
  logo into an emotion. Each found shape is a small mental rehearsal of the
  brand mark.
story_structure: >
  A series of ordinary people in unrelated places, each in a moment of
  desire, each noticing one found shape that echoes the brand mark, a quiet
  rhythm of sightings that grows more beautiful or more absurd, then the cut
  to the real product being made or eaten, and the logo with the tagline.
hook_methods: >
  Open on a found shape with a slightly lingering camera, such as a number in
  a notebook, two cranes on a building site, or reflections on a wet road,
  so the attentive viewer recognizes the brand before any character speaks.
product_role: >
  The product appears only as the payoff. Before that, the brand exists as
  its most iconic asset (logo letter, silhouette, color pair, sound), found
  in the world rather than placed in it.
suitable_categories_and_goals: >
  Brands with a famous, simple visual asset: fast food, soft drinks,
  sportswear swooshes and stripes, car grilles, pack silhouettes, delivery
  brands with a signature color. Craving, brand recall, and distinctive asset
  building.
unsuitable_cases: >
  New brands without an established mark, complex logos that cannot be found
  in nature, and categories where craving imagery is regulated or
  inappropriate, such as alcohol for young audiences, tobacco, and gambling.
common_mistakes: >
  Forcing shapes that only the creative team can see; pointing at every
  found shape with dialogue or arrows; showing the logo too early; too many
  sightings so the rhythm becomes a list; placing branded objects instead of
  finding shapes; forgetting the human state that motivates the seeing.

  Fast food: hungry people see golden arches in a rotated "3" in their notes, two excavator arms, a pair of arched windows and streetlight reflections on a wet street, then the kitchen and "i'm lovin' it".
  Sportswear: a runner waiting for a race sees the brand stripe in pedestrian crossings, shadows of railings and contrails in the sky.
  Chocolate bar: a tired office worker sees the bar's segmented grid in a calendar, a window façade and a parking lot.

# Brand Pareidolia

The commercial is a hunt for a shape. Desire makes the character, and the
viewer, find the brand mark in places where it was never designed to be.

## Why It Works

Recognition is pleasure. Every found shape lets the viewer complete the brand
in their own head, which builds memory more strongly than showing a logo.

## How To Execute

1. Identify the single brand asset simple enough to be found in the world.
2. Define the desire that sharpens the character's eye (hunger, thirst,
   fatigue, longing).
3. Find five to eight real-world shapes that echo the asset across different
   places, people, and times of day.
4. Frame each shape so it is visible without a pointer; let the camera
   linger a beat longer than usual.
5. Hold back the product and logo until the final ten seconds.
6. Close with the product being made or enjoyed, then the logo and tagline.
