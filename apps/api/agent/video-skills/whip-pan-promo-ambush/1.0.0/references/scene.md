# One threshold, two connected views

Establish the boundary view and the reverse interior view in one coherent
location. Show enough stable geometry to understand their relationship:
frame edges, aisle or corridor direction, ceiling lines, nearby wall planes,
and a consistent opening height.

Vehicle options include a cabin, train, bus, ferry, camper, or parked car.
Residential options include an entrance, living room, or apartment corridor.
Keep the selected moving or stationary state legible. Weather may be rain,
snow, hail, strong wind, dust, or fog; an ordinary knock requires none of them.

Design exterior movement and interior illumination as a coherent contrast.
For a home version, normal daylight or warm practical illumination may be
enough. A threshold can be closed, open, or visible through glass according
to the approved sequence; preserve that state until the planned change.

In the environment board, use complementary views to establish the boundary,
reverse direction, side relationship, and approach. Keep geometry consistent
while varying viewpoints and scales. Avoid a grid of near-identical doorways
that gives no usable spatial information.
