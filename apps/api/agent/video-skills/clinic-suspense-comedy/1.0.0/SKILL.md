# Clinic Suspense Comedy

Give an approved clinic comedy an oppressive opening and a legible, funny
release. The signature is contained consultation framing, delayed reaction
cuts and a freeze-frame finish. Emphasize the exchange, reaction timing and closing graphic hold.
Keep the approved product-related evidence legible throughout: conceal the
commercial source while preserving the gestures, surfaces and changes that
let the final reveal explain the exchange. Suspense comes from interpretation,
not from obscuring every functional clue.

## Contained consultation framing

Start with a stable spatial relationship, restrained color and quiet negative
space. Cool or neutral practical light, soft directional modeling and darker
room edges can suggest pressure. Keep eyes, gestures and relevant surfaces
readable; suspense does not require crushed shadows or constant background
blur. Moderate depth of field can retain useful environmental detail.

Stable shots can form shot/reverse-shot, reaction and detail coverage;
stability does not fix one viewpoint for the exchange. Preserve screen
direction and approved long takes. Let meaning motivate cuts; avoid constant
motion or premature comic emphasis.

## Delayed reaction cuts

Play the approved setup sincerely, with pauses on changes in meaning. An
approved late functional cutaway can briefly show a meaningful effect in
another environment while its commercial source stays unseen. Connect it
through framing, motion or sound, then return to the consultation before
the final exchange and instruction. A closing patient reaction or shift of
gaze is optional; include it only when it strengthens the approved scene.
A restrained delivery can contrast with a more exaggerated expression;
larger reactions are welcome once the joke has landed.

Light and color may become clearer at the release, but a drastic palette
change is optional. Comedy can emerge in the same cold room through a cut,
a stopped gesture or a suddenly relaxed posture. Keep functional inserts
readable and brief; save identifiable commercial detail shots for the final still.

## Freeze-frame finish

After the closing instruction or optional patient reaction, cut to the first
product appearance and brand reveal as a decisive still. Earlier functional
effects may appear; keep the item, identifying packaging and interface hidden,
including in backgrounds and accessories. Complete the clinic return before
this cut. Stage the still in the approved real-use setting, which may differ
from the clinic, with credible placement, scale and contact shadows. Retain
readable surroundings, sharp product detail and accurate material color.

Show the exact approved brand name in large, prominent typography from the
first frozen frame, simultaneously with the product, and hold both together.
Choose a clear typeface, contrast and placement suited to the brand and scene;
leave the hero shape unobscured and make benefit text secondary. Packaging
print or a small logo does not replace this prominent brand-name treatment.
Hold long enough for the preceding clues and commercial meaning to connect.
Exact freeze timing and final graphic treatment should be carried into the
available editing handoff; a planned effect is not an executed edit.
