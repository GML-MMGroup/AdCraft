# Script rhythm and tone

Apply a bright, playful, assured commercial tone to the selected story. Present
visual information through clear action and discoveries rather than a long
verbal explanation. Keep the approved product promise and creative mechanism.

Shape the visual rhythm as an inviting image, increasing movement or visual
revelation, and a calmer recognition finish. A stationary display can use
changing perspective and reveal timing instead of a flight sequence. Preserve
the story's required beats without adding a claw machine, a journey, or a
theme park solely to satisfy this style.

Write each beat with a clear visual focus and a motivated transition. Allow
moments where the audience can recognize the subject before the next visual
surprise. Keep approved narration and on-screen wording concise enough to fit
the existing brief and reading time.

Describe the closing image with a legible silhouette, quieter surroundings,
and a confident pause. Keep any spectacle tied to the chosen brand tone;
avoid turning every event into a joke or adding exaggerated efficacy claims.
