# Exchange, response and hold

Establish the approved room geography, then use coherent shot/reverse-shot
and selective close-ups. Give the nurse a legible position and a clear eyeline
when addressed. Each shot should expose new evidence or a meaningful response;
vary scale and subject purpose instead of repeating nearly identical portraits.
Distinguish stable individual shots from a fixed viewpoint across the segment.
Keep an explicitly approved long take; the grid imposes no cut count.

Show speech through acting and eyelines, without rendering dialogue subtitles,
speech bubbles or quoted spoken lines. Packaging print, environmental signs
and other authored non-dialogue text remain allowed at their approved moment;
this does not advance the final product and brand reveal.

An approved late functional passage may leave the clinic to show an effect,
supporting mechanism or result, while the advertised item stays out of view.
Link it to the patient's account and explicitly plan the return to the same
clinic for the final exchange. Then let the doctor's gaze lead toward the
nurse before the instruction. If the story includes a closing patient reaction,
give its expression or gesture a readable close or medium view. Otherwise,
cut directly from the final dialogue to the brand freeze; no extra reaction
panel or hold is required. In either case, proceed to the
final frozen brand frame: the marketed item appears for the first time here.
Every preceding panel excludes that item, its recognizable packaging or
identifying interface, and brand text, including screens and reflections.
Judge visibility against the advertised subject: a supporting mechanism is
allowed only when it does not itself reveal the item being sold.
A reference image supplied as input is not a requirement to depict it in a shot.
Compose the final frame in the product's approved real-use environment,
with believable placement and recognizable context. This is separate from
any earlier functional passage. Reserve prominent space for the exact brand name in
large type, visible from the first frozen frame alongside the product;
place the approved benefit second in the visual hierarchy. Keep early product
beauty shots and identifiable-item demonstrations out of the functional passage.

Retain the platform's nine-panel storyboard format and map panels to the
current segment's beats; a grid is not automatically the entire story. Only
the concluding segment contains the brand freeze. Keep identities, eyelines
and room layout consistent across the consultation and its return; distinguish
functional and closing environments from that location. Record the freeze and brand-title
onset as the same moment for editing.
