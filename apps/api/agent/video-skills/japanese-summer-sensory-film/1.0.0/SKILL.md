# Japanese Summer Sensory Film

Build atmosphere through seasonal observation, tactile sensory inserts,
natural product use, and a breathing hero finish. The Japanese-advertising
sensibility lies in noticing everyday sensation and giving it space, not in
requiring Japanese text, a particular location, an ethnicity, or animation.

## Light and surfaces

Keep summer light luminous and directional: sun sparkles, translucent edges,
gentle flare, warm skin, and cooler shade. The reference uses clear cyan water,
deep blue sky, cloud white, yellow liquid, and green accents. Retain that
fresh separation while adapting around the actual item and setting. A shaded
interior can use moving window light and airy neutrals instead of a seascape.
Bright summer color can be rich; a faded pastel filter is not mandatory.

Show believable skin, damp hair, fabric, glass, liquid, and ground texture.
Use condensation only on plausibly chilled surfaces and bubbles only for a
product that supports them. Keep highlights from erasing packaging or faces.
Photographic detail and soft background separation take priority over heavy
grain, plastic smoothing, decorative scratches, or illustrated surfaces.

## Atmosphere and movement

Select one or two connected sensory events: water meeting fingertips, a breeze
in fabric, dappled light, a short run, or an unhurried pause. The active route
may use a loose turn, a walk, or a playful gesture. The quiet route may use a
window-side rest, a small reach, or an everyday preparation. Both should feel
lived rather than continuously posed.

Alternate an environmental view with a tactile close-up, a use action, and a
human response when the cast calls for it. Gentle tracking, a modest handheld
drift, a motivated low view, or a held close-up are options. Let movement resolve
before a calm hero view. Wind, highlights, water, or background activity can
keep that ending alive while the product remains legible.

## Product and cast continuity

The actual use earns the atmosphere. Preserve grip, contact, material behavior,
packaging orientation, and state before and after use. Cast size follows the
approved brief and references. Keep distinct identities and their assigned
items stable in group coverage; partial hands belong to the established cast.

A 15-30 second ad is the intended use case, not a provider duration rule.
Final footage is photographic live action. Intermediate assets retain their
own rendering and composition contracts; this skill adds no blanket face
illustration requirement. Exact typography, overlays, and finishing belong
to the approved production handoff.
