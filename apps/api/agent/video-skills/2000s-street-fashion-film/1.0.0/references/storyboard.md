# Look, detail, city, and return

Use the required nine-panel storyboard to communicate the approved sequence,
not nine compulsory cuts. Allocate panels to distinct visual information:
a readable look, a movement beat, a material detail, a candid portrait, and
a connected street view as the narrative needs. Mix scales and viewpoints;
a grid of nearly identical walking mid-shots loses the reference's variety.

For each panel, name visible identities, their screen positions, the hero
item, the direction of movement, and the owner of hands or foreground shoulders.
Keep the same model in successive panels without combining those appearances
into several bodies in one frame. With several models, preserve independent
faces and look assignments; every reference need not appear in every panel.

Plan occlusions, glances, reflected views, or matching motion as transitions
without treating the grid as literal final camera placement. Mark which beats
need a clean fashion read and which allow a short soft or motion-blurred bridge.
Give material inserts enough scale to distinguish the actual item.

Keep panels free of typography. Reserve an ending composition with breathing
room and carry exact brand wording, timing, and placement into the editing
handoff. A facade or a dark graphic ending is optional, not a required location.
