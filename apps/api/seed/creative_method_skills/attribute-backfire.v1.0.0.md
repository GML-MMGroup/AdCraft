core_principle: >
  Stage a sincere, high-stakes genre moment, such as a desert survival, a cliff
  rescue, or a long-distance love scene, and play it completely straight. At
  the peak, the product is used to help, and its one attribute works so much
  better than reason allows that it wrecks the moment. The product does not
  fail; it over-delivers, and the over-delivery is the proof.
consumer_psychology: >
  Benefit-by-catastrophe: a disaster caused by too much of a quality is more
  memorable and more believable as exaggeration than a neat demonstration.
  The sincere setup lowers the viewer's guard, so the backfire lands as a
  genuine surprise, and the stated attribute on the end card becomes the
  explanation the viewer was looking for.
story_structure: >
  A committed genre setup with real stakes and earnest performances, the
  product introduced as a reasonable help at the emotional peak, the backfire
  where the attribute overshoots and destroys the plan, an optional second
  attempt that uses the same attribute on purpose and escalates the disaster,
  and a product card that names the attribute in two to four words.
hook_methods: >
  Open inside the crisis with genre signals turned up: survival music, sweat,
  a map, a clasped hand over a drop, a phone call at night. The first seconds
  promise a blockbuster or a melodrama, not an ad.
product_role: >
  The product is the cause of the twist, never a side prop. Exactly one
  attribute (absorbency, smoothness, volume, mint strength, grip) is pushed
  to a physical result at the scale of the genre: an oasis drained, a map set
  on fire, a helicopter hit, a person blown off her feet.
suitable_categories_and_goals: >
  Everyday consumables with one clear functional attribute: personal care,
  hygiene, hair care, mints and gum, cleaning products, snacks, tape and
  glue, batteries. Awareness, shareability, and series formats where the same
  characters and genre return for each product.
unsuitable_cases: >
  Products whose attribute is safety-critical, so that the backfire reads as
  a defect claim: medicine, car brakes, baby products, financial services.
  Prestige luxury, and brands whose audience would read the disaster as harm
  to a real person.
common_mistakes: >
  Winking at the camera before the backfire, so the setup is never sincere;
  a backfire unrelated to the stated attribute; several attributes in one
  film; a disaster that injures or humiliates someone in a way that stops the
  laugh; ending on the disaster without a product card that names the
  attribute; an end card that claims the result literally.

# Attribute Backfire

The commercial borrows the seriousness of a genre and then lets the product
win too hard. The bigger and more sincere the setup, the funnier and clearer
the over-delivery.

## Why It Works

A neat demonstration is easy to ignore; a catastrophe with an obvious cause is
not. Because the product works rather than fails, the joke is also the claim,
and the viewer replays the moment to see the attribute do it.

## How To Execute

1. Pick one attribute and the genre in which it would be most out of scale.
2. Play the genre setup straight: real stakes, earnest acting, genre music.
3. Introduce the product as a reasonable help at the emotional peak.
4. Let the attribute overshoot into a physical result at blockbuster scale.
5. Optionally insert a quick product card, then return for a second, bigger
   backfire that uses the same attribute on purpose.
6. Close on a product card that names the attribute in two to four words;
   for fictional or spec brands add a small "fictional brand" note.
7. Keep the format reusable: same characters and genre, a new product and
   attribute each episode.
