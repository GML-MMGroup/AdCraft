# Movement and native scene sound

In suspense passages, favor a locked view or small controlled approach. Let
the approved hand pause, forward lean, glance, or breath provide motion.
At the reveal, expose the source cleanly and allow the ordinary action to
complete. Keep geometry, contact, object count, and identity stable across
the change in framing or illumination.

For authorized dialogue, preserve the approved words, speaker assignment,
and restrained delivery with space for the listener's reaction. Do not add
unrequested speech or on-screen captions. When native ambient sound and foley
are enabled, use the real source: motor onset, a switch click, fabric contact,
liquid movement, or an interface response appropriate to the selected product.
Keep synchronous contact sounds aligned with visible events.

A withheld-source cue may sound unusually prominent in a sparse environment;
retain recognizable timbre and rhythm when the source is revealed. Do not
turn a device into an animal growl or manufacture claims of perfect silence.
Honor silent segments. Do not request background music in video generation;
the BGM role owns the instrumental bed and any tonal score transition.
