# Freeze the incident; move the camera

Write segment-local intervals plus incoming and outgoing states. Show ordinary
speed, exact contact, body response and support release in that order. At the
freeze entry lock each object's world position, orientation, cap/hinge state,
liquid shape and droplet positions until time resumes. Apparent angle and
reflections change because the camera moves, not because a cap opens again or
the object spins. Use minute drift only for an approved extreme-slow variant.

Map each hero's depth and lateral position relative to the protagonist. Keep the
background pose nearly fixed. Travel through that mapped space, giving one sharp
hero a readable detail at a time; other objects still exist out of focus or out
of frame. State camera start, direction, endpoint and focus. Use changing overlap
and parallax, not a static zoom. Prefer a continuous feasible path for a frozen
fly-through. If controlled cuts are approved, keep the same event state across
them. Return to a wider view before resuming the original downward trajectories;
show landing contact, settling and the owner's readable face, not only a back.

TEN-SECOND EXAMPLE, only for an approved ten-second standalone segment:
'Use the supplied wearer, glasses, open cup and bag identities in one walkway.
0-2.3s: track backward low, rising to the face; show glasses low on the nose,
a hand gripping the cup and the bag strap resting near the shoulder edge.
2.3-2.7s: a passerby's outer shoulder visibly brushes the wearer; the torso
recoils, the grip opens, the glasses slip and the strap slides off its support.
2.7-4.5s: freeze the released glasses just ahead of the face; move from their
front rim toward the real hinge, reveal parallax and hold the hinge sharp.
4.5-6.2s: move to the same cup's mapped position; inspect its rim and a few
sourced droplets with a short sideward move. 6.2-7.8s: inspect the same bag's
seam and hardware by a limited arc, then ease back to the wearer. Keep every
object singular and its post-release state fixed. 7.8-10s: restore ordinary
gravity; those objects land and settle on the visible walkway; show the
surprised face. Remove liquid effects when the supplied container is sealed.'

FIFTEEN-SECOND EXAMPLE, only when approved, for a frozen-space fly-through:
'0-3s: footwear-to-face tracking establishes the referenced glasses, handheld
phone and cup. 3-5s: a passing shoulder brushes the upper arm; show recoil and
release, then freeze. 5-7s: pass the glasses rim; 7-9s: slide alongside the cup;
9-11s: reach the phone's real camera cluster. Each stays in its mapped position
with the same shape and opening state; only the camera passes them in order.
11-13s: pull back to a front three-quarter medium view, resume time and follow
the fall. 13-15s: settle on the landing and tilt to the owner's startled face.'

FOUR-SECOND EXAMPLE, one hero and one detail:
'0-0.8s: one supplied capped bottle rests on a carried tray. 0.8-1.1s: the tray
edge catches a sleeve; show tilt and loss of support. 1.1-3.1s: freeze the bottle
in midair; slide from front three-quarter view toward its print and edge highlight.
Keep the same bottle, closed cap and background pose. 3.1-4s: time resumes;
it lands on the previously visible towel, settles, and the owner reacts.
The cap stays closed; no liquid appears.' Adapt support and surface to the brief.

Substitute the current approved inventory; a single product needs no extra props.
These are internal shots, not separate short generation nodes. A continuation
executes only its assigned phase. Keep speech and subtitle settings intact.
Use footfalls, a brief contact accent, sparse suspended movement and precise
landing foley; BGM is separate. Check causal order and inventory in text before
returning, without an extra visual-model call.
