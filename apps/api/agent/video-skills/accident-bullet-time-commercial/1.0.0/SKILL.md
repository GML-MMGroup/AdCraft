# Accident Bullet-Time Commercial

Make the speed contrast unmistakable: ordinary movement, a readable contact,
then near-frozen products explored by an actively moving camera, followed by
resumed gravity. Preserve this event order across the whole film. Apply only
the assigned portion within each generation segment; do not replay the impact
or finish every segment with another landing.

Apply event choreography to Script, Storyboard and Video work. Static asset
roles establish reusable appearance and geometry; use their current reference
format without staging the accident inside a reference asset.

## Defining requirements

1. Establish the supplied subject and product before the mishap. Show the actual
contact or support failure, body response and release in that order. Products
remain held or attached until their support visibly changes.
2. Use a contact-triggered speed ramp. During the suspended interval the object
holds its post-release position, orientation and component state while the
camera travels. Use minute drift only for an approved extreme-slow variant. Normal-speed product spinning, a uniform slow-motion walk, and a motionless
poster do not deliver this treatment.
3. Use parallax-led product inspection: a short push, lateral slide or limited
arc changes foreground/background overlap and reveals a real surface or edge.
Keep one sharp hero per close-up. A digital zoom or rack focus alone is insufficient.
4. Maintain one continuous incident: the same inventory, wardrobe, product
geometry, release position, light, background landmarks and downward destination.
Several views of one item never create additional copies. A controlled cut may
join compatible views; it must not teleport the product into a studio.
5. Return to gravity and show the resulting landing, spill, catch or reaction
at the planned ending. Resolve the same trajectories instead of switching to
an unrelated static packshot.

## Image and motion treatment

Favor tangible commercial photography, readable material texture, directional
highlights, shallow but sufficient focus, and a real location behind the hero.
Use high-shutter clarity as an aesthetic cue during near-frozen action, with
brief natural blur at the trigger and return. This is not a required output
frame rate. Keep critical print, edge and feature geometry legible. Maintain an
explicitly requested animation medium while preserving these time and camera
relationships; reference photos do not override that medium.

A low tracking opening that rises from footwear to the face works well when
relevant. During the product tour, favor limited rotation and a short camera
arc over a full orbit through unsupported surfaces. Focus transitions follow
the planned hero order. Approved cuts are often more controllable than forcing
one impossible unbroken flight through every object.

## Prompt authority

Put contact, release, speed phases, camera path and final state into the visible
storyboard and Video prompts. Name the reference-backed identity and product
features; the words 'bullet time' alone are insufficient. Preserve the approved
segment duration, grid count and provider capabilities. Short internal shots
share valid generation windows; a four-panel segment remains four seconds.
Use one hero for a short window rather than deleting the cause or consequence.
