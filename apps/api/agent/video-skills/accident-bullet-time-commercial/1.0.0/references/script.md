# One product, several belongings, one complete accident

Use the advertised product plus two distinct plausible supporting belongings,
chosen through the normal prop-design flow. An approved product set can supply
real separate items. Three surfaces of one object are not three belongings.
Keep additional approved items in the inventory and account for their landing.

For an open cast, list the protagonist as the designed identity and the anonymous
passerby separately as background action, without a Character asset or roster
entry. Preserve approved cast and later speaking/recurring roles; do not invent
them to justify a second identity. Respect explicit total-person restrictions.
Establish independent walking routes before contact; gaze and stride follow each
destination. Opposing passage or approaching from behind with relative speed
and narrowing clearance may cause the brush. A back/shoulder glimpse suffices;
natural frontal visibility is allowed, without an introduction beat. Carry this
distinction into production inventory and handoff, not just visual prose.
Show initial support before contact. The quick shoulder brush causes slight
backward recoil, open palms and the original items tossing forward and upward,
not an intentional throw. Stretch the airborne instant, not the contact.

Map each item's support, release, frozen position and landing. Respect available
hands, attachments and world rules. Preserve expressive upward release rather
than a downward slip or forward head dip. Freeze the slight backward lean,
open empty palms and separated items near the crest. Each item has one location;
reference panels show states of the same item, not copies.
The vacated support stays empty until an authored pickup or reattachment. Keep
attached parts with their object and retain offscreen states through landing.
Freeze head and gaze with the backward-leaning body; new reactions begin when
time resumes. The carrier looks toward the airborne items rather than the ground.

Preserve the sequence: footwear tracking rises to wearer and belongings; visible
passing contact causes release; everything freezes together; the camera visits
A, B and C; it pulls back; time resumes, all objects land and settle, and the
wearer reacts. The advertised product receives the strongest attention. Preserve
approved speech and claims; a drop or intact landing does not prove durability.

Keep the first-frame footwear reveal through all objects landing in one video
segment, even in a long advertisement. Start_state opens on feet with items
still held or worn; the camera rises to show initial support before contact.
Do not assign the opening track, rise or setup to another node. Budget this
whole interval before any post-landing story or brand payoff. The
final two to three seconds cover resumed fall, every ground contact and settling.
End_state records all grounded belongings and body pose so a later node starts
there rather than repeating the fall. Honor the fifteen-second generation cap
and confirmed total for the opening and incident together. Shorten optional holds
before compromising the reveal, cause, travel or descent; disclose incompatible
timing without adding a rejection gate or silently extending time.

Preserve confirmed visit order; supporting details are brief, with the product's
strongest material view or scale change wherever it falls in that order. Fabric,
crumbs or condensation follow the event; a splash needs an open vessel. Preserve
actual materials and closures.

Give the tour fast approaches, readable brief inspections and fast departures.
Leave camera geometry to Storyboard and Video, carrying that emphasis and the
footwear-to-landing interval into narrative_goal and production_handoff. Reactions
follow the approved tone; a body fall is optional. Review inventory, causality and
handoff in this call, without an added paid visual-model check.
