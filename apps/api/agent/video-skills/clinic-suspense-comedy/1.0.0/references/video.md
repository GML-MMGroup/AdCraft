# Controlled motion and a dry release

During the approved setup, keep each shot stable or use restrained movement
with small, purposeful gestures. Stability within a shot does not mean one
viewpoint for the whole exchange. Follow authored shot/reverse-shot, nurse
reactions and evidence inserts with motivated cuts; do not reduce them to
same-axis crops of a group shot. Preserve an explicitly approved long take
or fixed viewpoint without imposing a cut count. Preserve speaker assignment,
face identity, room geography and screen direction. Make the verbal turn readable through performance, then allow the reaction
to land before cutting away. The approved doctor-to-nurse line needs a clear
turn or eyeline toward the nurse, not an accidental address to the patient.

Keep the advertised item absent until the final still, including during a
functional cutaway. Exclude its packaging, identifying interface, recognizable
worn form and brand text even when its reference is supplied as input. Native
speech and sound also withhold its name and brand; late functional cues may
make the benefit or category understandable.

When authored, render the late external passage as a brief, causally clear
effect, supporting mechanism or result. Keep the marketed item off screen,
including reflections and background views. Use the approved sound or speech
bridge, then return to the established clinic for the final exchange and the
doctor's instruction. If a segment ends during the insert, preserve this return
in the next segment rather than jumping straight to the brand still.

If the approved sequence includes a patient reaction after the doctor's
instruction, perform it with
a clear onset and brief hold: its smile, pause or small gesture should echo
the character's scripted unusual state. Keep facial identity stable and the
action readable instead of turning it into arbitrary face distortion. When
the reaction is omitted, move directly from the final dialogue to the brand
still; do not invent a smile, gesture or extra pause to fill the gap. This
choice is independent of whether a functional cutaway is included.

For the final segment, specify the cut from the final dialogue or chosen reaction to the
brand still as the first product appearance. Use the approved real-use
environment and credible product placement, rather than automatically keeping
the clinic background. Keep the setting readable and the product stable.
The exact approved brand name appears in large, prominent text on the first
frozen frame, at the same time as the product, and remains through the hold.
Keep benefit text secondary and legible so this frame explains the earlier clues.
Carry the selected frame and hold intent into the available editing handoff
for an exact freeze; prompting alone does not guarantee a literal held frame.
Earlier segments continue their local action without repeating the end card.

Honor native audio settings. When enabled, keep the approved dialogue clear
and synchronize restrained room sounds with visible actions. A short comic
sound accent is optional at the payoff, not a replacement for performance.
Leave the instrumental score to the separate BGM role.

Respect generate_subtitles: when enabled, request ordinary-size white speech
text with a black outline, no background box, at the lower center; when off,
do not request speech subtitles. The final approved brand graphic is separate
from dialogue subtitles: its large brand-name requirement applies even when
generate_subtitles is off and does not inherit ordinary subtitle sizing.
Provide its exact wording, composition and simultaneous onset with the freeze
to the available finishing handoff.
