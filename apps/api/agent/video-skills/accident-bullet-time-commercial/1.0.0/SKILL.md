# Accident Bullet-Time Commercial

Use a contact-triggered speed ramp for parallax-led product inspection.
Keep the approved story, identities, inventory and medium.

## Write the release into the action

Match every released item to its own support, including the advertised product.
Write the separation and resulting vacancy together at impact. For example:
phone clears right fingertips, right palm empty; bottle clears left fingertips,
left palm empty; glasses clear the nose and ears, face bare. Use the actual items
and sides. At freeze and pullback keep each visible support in that state.
Before returning, compare the written action to the release list and fill any
missing pair in the action itself.

Contact starts slight backward recoil, surprise, opening hands, outward/upward
release and camera approach together. Keep the backward tilt explicit. Inspect
airborne originals through pullback; restore gravity before their first landing.

Keep the first-frame footwear reveal through all objects landing in one video
segment, within the fifteen-second cap and confirmed total. Reserve two to three
seconds for landing. Later story or payoff follows the grounded state.

## Establish cause and transfer

Track back at shoe height and rise in one take. Keep the approved gait through
contact: for a walk, keep one foot grounded and the same cadence as framing
widens. Use camera speed and street activity for urgency. Establish each item's
ordinary support unless the story specifies otherwise. Choose independent routes;
shoulders meet at one depth, fabric compresses, torso deflects backward. The
anonymous extra keeps their destination and gaze.

Keep contact and asymmetric recovery near normal speed. As the camera approaches
A, show support loss along the moving view. Each original follows an outward/upward
arc from its support before slowing. Continue without waiting for a group frame.

## Traverse one frozen space

Use the product plus two distinct supporting items unless the approved set
supplies those slots. Stage approved A/B/C near to far in a bounded depth corridor
with support-derived height and lateral offsets. Give EVERY item a readable
partial camera orbit: front/three-quarter to side detail, with background parallax.
Join each arc tangentially to the next approach; the outgoing edge and already
visible next target share the bridge. Advance overall through depth while curving
locally around each fixed item. Accelerate between visits, slow while circling;
roll/tilt alone is not an orbit. Give the product the strongest view. Keep the
final pullback, resumed gravity and settling.

## Preserve states across views

Surprise starts with contact: widened eyes, lifted brows and slightly parted lips.
As the interrupted action slows into its airborne crest, freeze that expression,
body, head and gaze through inspection/pullback; retain surprise through settling.
Freeze object geometry, closures and displaced material in world space.
Pair each airborne original with its exposed attachment point: e.g. the scarf
clears the collar, which stays visible in later body views and pullback until
reattachment. Keep unrelated accessories in place. Flag stale reference poses.
A splash needs an open source; a drop is not durability proof.

The grid is an off-screen story-process reference, not a first/last-frame target
or required cut list. Each panel is one instant with one location per original.
All inspection views remain airborne even when a design reference rests on a
surface. The final panel alone shows the settled result. Start Video directly
in the full-frame scene. Preserve approved speech and subtitle controls.
