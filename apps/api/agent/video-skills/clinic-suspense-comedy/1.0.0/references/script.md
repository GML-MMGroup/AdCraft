# Dialogue rhythm and comic timing

Pace the approved consultation as a serious exchange. Leave a short silence
after an ambiguous answer and let the listener's interrupted gesture register.
Avoid an early wink, playful cadence or explanatory aside that spoils the
information order. Give each answer a distinct emotional intention.

Separate the verbal turn from the product reveal. Play the late answer and
comic response without naming the advertised item or brand. A late functional
insert may clarify the experience or suggest the category. If used, bridge it
with the approved patient line or a purposeful pause, and return to the room
for the final exchange. The final still identifies the commercial source.
For the approved doctor-to-nurse instruction, specify the addressee. A closing
patient reaction is optional: choose it for a useful comic or character beat,
or move directly from the final dialogue to the brand freeze. If included,
give the performer a specific
intention, gesture and timing tied to the earlier exchange: a slightly
incongruous smile, held gaze or small repeated action can convey the fictional
character's established unusual state. Keep it distinct from a routine happy
endorsement; restrained or exaggerated expression can work. Do not add a new
diagnosis or treat a smile as a universal clinical sign.
Do not change the selected ending or add another joke after the brand close.
Fit speech, pauses and a readable final hold to the supplied runtime. The
story owns the actual words, evidence and cast; this reference owns delivery.
