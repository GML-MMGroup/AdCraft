# State-based transitions in the nine-panel grid

Keep the required nine-panel format. Describe the approved sequence with
distinct departure, contact, threshold, arrival, detail, and closing information
as needed; nine cells do not require nine cuts or nine different worlds.

For each transition, record the visible trigger, unchanged identity and item,
screen direction, selected continuity anchor, changed environment, and arrival
state. Show the consequence of the world rule instead of filling panels with
unrelated impossible images. Plan any scale change with a readable comparison.

Specify who appears, where each body stands or sits, who owns interacting hands,
and which item each model carries. Repeated views across panels represent the
same cast, not several simultaneous bodies. An orbit or reverse view must not
retain a second model at the prior position.

Give tactile detail a visible connection to the supplied item. Plan enough
space and time for stable geometry and product recognition at the ending.
Grid framing remains guidance rather than a forced camera match. Keep panels
text-free and carry exact typography, effects timing, and any overlays in the
production handoff.
