# Witness and presenter contrast

Set cast size and identities from the approved brief. A solitary viewpoint,
one visible resident, a small group, or an ensemble can supply the reaction.
The visitor is a distinct identity unless the approved story deliberately
assigns an existing cast member that role.

Define the performance contrast: a startled witness and an enthusiastic seller,
a worried group and an oddly composed visitor, or a quiet resident and a
cheerfully urgent pitch. Raised brows, an open mouth, forceful pointing, and
large but performable expressions are welcome when they serve the joke.
Do not force every performer into a deadpan face.

Choose wardrobe, age presentation, build, and styling for the campaign.
The source's wet, muscular, shirtless seller is one option, not a requirement.
Keep faces, hair, outfits, body positions, and assigned hand actions stable
through the two camera moves. Each identity occupies one physical location
at a time unless an explicit story device establishes otherwise.

Identity references follow their current image format and rendering contract;
this treatment adds no universal illustrated-face requirement.
