# Moving-image treatment

Translate each approved segment into a starting state, one primary action,
one dominant camera path, and a precise ending state. Use smooth tracking,
controlled push-ins, low sweeping travel, or a restrained orbit. Keep the
horizon and screen direction understandable during speed changes.

Make nearby framing elements move faster than distant architecture to create
parallax. Keep motion blur mainly on fast background edges; protect the hero
silhouette and important recognition beats. Avoid aggressive shake, repeated
speed ramps, fisheye stretching, and continuous camera spinning.

Carry position, orientation, angular velocity, scale, and light across hidden
joins. Use an actual foreground occluder or darker passage from the approved
scene. Emerging behind an occluder must not change the destination or teleport
the subject. Show clearance around glass, frames, and other solid surfaces.

For floating or oversized action already in the story, give motion a readable
trajectory and gradual acceleration. Maintain realistic material response
throughout. If a landing exists, decelerate before contact, establish contact
shadow, and use only a small material-appropriate settling movement.

Resolve to a calmer hero composition with stable form and readable approved
branding. Hold long enough for recognition within the specified segment.
Preserve the supplied frame shape, timing, and representation requirements.

When the existing brief requests native environmental sound, sparse contact,
air movement, and soft arrival cues may follow the visible action. Keep these
restrained and physically timed; do not add them when the brief is silent.
