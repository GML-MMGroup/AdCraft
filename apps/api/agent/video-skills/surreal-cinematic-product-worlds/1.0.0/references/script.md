# An understandable trigger and a recoverable product

Open on a specific everyday situation with the supplied item already involved.
Give the surreal change an action the audience can follow. Tie the new world
to the approved product quality, shape, texture, or use; a generic fantasy
destination would weaken the commercial idea.

Keep a short film focused on one dominant altered rule. A longer brief can
develop anticipation, arrival, and response without requiring multiple worlds.
Cast size, relationships, and emotional tone come from the approved narrative.
A fatigued office worker is a source example, not the default protagonist.

Retain or restore ordinary use scale and an honest interaction before the hero
finish. A real use action may be the trigger and need not be repeated later.
The ending can remain in the extraordinary setting if the product and its
relationship to the user are clear.

Preserve requested dialogue or narration; a visual idea can also work without
explanatory speech. Give the final item time to be recognized. Supply exact
approved brand wording and any CTA in the designated handoff, separating
expressive world changes from claims about efficacy or real-world capability.
