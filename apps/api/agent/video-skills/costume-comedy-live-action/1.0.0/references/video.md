# Real actors, heightened comic performance

Render final footage as live-action costume advertising: natural facial
anatomy, photographic skin, real hair, weighted garments, and dimensional
sets. Convert locally illustrated human-face references into the requested
live-action appearance while preserving identity. Do not retain anime lines,
cel shading, chibi bodies, plastic cartoon skin, or animated eye effects.

Keep acting boldly comic. A commanding glare can snap into a startled recoil;
skepticism can become widened eyes, an open-mouthed pause, an emphatic double
take, and a delighted grin. Large gestures and abrupt changes of posture are
welcome when motivated by the approved beat. Preserve stable anatomy and
recognizable identity throughout. Realism governs rendering, not emotional
restraint; do not flatten the source's comedy into solemn historical drama.

Use a controlled frame for status and a quick push-in or reaction cut for
the comic turn. Isolate the product action with tangible contact and stable
grip. For food, keep bite size, chewing, sauce, and remaining portions
physically continuous. For other items, show the actual approved operation.
Let sleeves, jewelry, and headwear respond naturally to movement.

Settle into shared delight or the approved payoff, then a readable product
finish. Preserve the real packaging and supported spoken benefit. Product
appearance must not change when the scene becomes a sales presentation.

Native audio prioritizes intelligible dialogue, consistent voices, modest
room ambience, and synchronous action foley. Short comic punctuation may
accent a double take or sudden change of mood; avoid covering the line or
every gesture. Exclude background music from the video prompt; the BGM role
owns it. Respect the selected audio mode and leave sales graphics to the
approved editing plan unless the current output contract explicitly allows them.
