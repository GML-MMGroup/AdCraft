# The two-whip reveal in nine panels

Retain the required nine-panel storyboard. Its panels can show key states
within the signature coverage: A, the disturbance at the boundary; A-to-B,
the first whip; B, a readable reaction; B-to-A-prime, the return whip;
A-prime, the unexpected presenter and item; then a settled offer view.
Nine panels are not nine compulsory cuts or repeated camera swings.

For each whip, record start framing, camera-relative travel direction,
destination framing, and the landmark that confirms arrival. Indicate the
brief blur interval without using blur to hide a change of identity or place.
Keep the threshold in the same world position when returning to it.

Name each visible cast member, their position, gaze, and hand ownership.
The visitor's supporting hand and presenting hand must remain distinct;
a foreground shoulder belongs to the established cast, not an extra duplicate.
Give the item a readable scale and keep its orientation consistent.

Hold enough clear coverage after the reveal for the pitch and actual offer.
Plan portrait-safe separation of face, merchandise, and later typography,
adapting to the approved frame shape. Keep panels free of text and carry exact
event wording, price, conditions, and CTA placement into the editing handoff.
